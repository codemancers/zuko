---
title: Prospect Lifecycle
description: Terminology, states, events, and transition rules that govern a person's journey from outbound target to closed deal.
---

Zuko tracks a person across two very different phases of a sales motion: the **outbound** phase, where we reach out and most people never answer, and the **CRM** phase, where a human relationship exists and is worth managing. Collapsing the two into one record makes both worse — outbound noise pollutes the CRM, and CRM semantics do not survive bulk enrolment.

This page defines the vocabulary, the states, the events that drive them, and the rules for moving between them. The machine-readable version of everything here lives in `libs/sales/src/lib/constants/prospects.ts` and is exported from `@zuko/sales` — code must import it rather than re-declare string literals.

## Terminology

| Term                    | Means                                                                                              | Lives in             |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **ICP Profile**         | The definition of who we want to reach — filters, notes, positioning.                              | `IcpProfile`         |
| **Prospect**            | A person we intend to contact. Sourced from a provider or added manually. No relationship yet.     | `Prospect`           |
| **Campaign**            | An outbound sequence, usually backed by a provider sequence (Apollo).                              | `Campaign`           |
| **Campaign Membership** | One prospect's participation in one campaign. Carries campaign state, engagement, and disposition. | `CampaignMembership` |
| **Lead**                | A prospect who showed intent and was promoted. Owned by a human, worked by hand.                   | `Lead`               |
| **Contact / Company**   | CRM records for people and organizations we have a real relationship with.                         | `Contact`, `Company` |
| **Deal**                | The revenue opportunity opened on conversion.                                                      | `Deal`               |

The line to hold: **a prospect is a target, a lead is a conversation.** Automation owns prospects; people own leads.

<Note>
  `Prospect` and `CampaignMembership` do not exist yet — they are introduced by
  the data-model task that follows this one. Until then, `Lead` carries both
  meanings and campaign recipients live only in Apollo.
</Note>

## Events are not states

The distinction that keeps this model honest:

- An **event** is something that happened at a point in time. It is immutable, appended to history, and never overwritten. `message_opened`, `reply_received`, `message_bounced`.
- A **state** is what is true right now. It is derived from events, and it changes.

Three opens and a click are three events and a click — they are not a state. They are worth storing and worth scoring, but nothing about the membership changed: the person still has not answered. Only a reply moves `contacted` to `responded`.

Keeping the two apart means campaign activity can be re-processed from history without corrupting standing, and it stops "opened twice" from being mistaken for interest.

## The three axes of a membership

A membership answers three independent questions, and conflating them is the usual modelling mistake. Someone can be `completed` on the campaign axis, `no_response` on the engagement axis, and `nurture` on the disposition axis — all at once.

| Axis            | Question                                    | Values                                                                      |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Campaign**    | Where are they in the campaign's mechanics? | `identified` · `enrolled` · `active` · `completed` · `removed`              |
| **Engagement**  | How have they responded?                    | `not_contacted` · `contacted` · `responded` · `no_response` · `unreachable` |
| **Disposition** | What did we conclude about them?            | `interested` · `nurture` · `disqualified` · `opted_out`                     |

### Campaign state

| State        | Means                                                                    |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `identified` | Selected for the campaign, not yet handed to the provider.               |
| `enrolled`   | Added to the sequence, nothing sent yet.                                 |
| `active`     | The sequence is running.                                                 |
| `completed`  | The sequence finished on its own. **Terminal.**                          |
| `removed`    | Pulled out early — opt-out, bounce, promotion, or by hand. **Terminal.** |

```
  ┌────────────┐      ┌──────────┐      ┌────────┐      ┌───────────┐
  │ identified │─────►│ enrolled │─────►│ active │─────►│ completed │
  └─────┬──────┘      └────┬─────┘      └───┬────┘      └───────────┘
        │                  │                │
        └──────────────────┴────────────────┴──────────►  removed
                    (opt-out · bounce · promotion · manual)
```

### Engagement state

| State           | Means                                                          |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `not_contacted` | Enrolled, nothing delivered yet.                               |
| `contacted`     | At least one message delivered.                                |
| `responded`     | They answered. **Terminal.**                                   |
| `no_response`   | The sequence ended in silence. Reversible by a late reply.     |
| `unreachable`   | Hard bounce, invalid address, no usable channel. **Terminal.** |

```
  ┌───────────────┐      ┌───────────┐      ┌───────────┐
  │ not_contacted │─────►│ contacted │─────►│ responded │
  └───────┬───────┘      └─────┬─────┘      └───────────┘
          │                    │                  ▲
          │                    ▼                  │ late reply
          │              ┌─────────────┐          │
          │              │ no_response │──────────┘
          │              └─────────────┘
          └────────────────────┴──────────────────► unreachable
```

`no_response` is deliberately not terminal. People reply weeks after a sequence gives up, and that reply is worth as much as a prompt one.

### Disposition

The disposition is our conclusion, and it is the bridge from campaign activity to prospect standing. A membership must carry one once its campaign state is terminal.

| Disposition    | Means                                    | Prospect becomes |
| -------------- | ---------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| `interested`   | Positive intent — worth a human.         | `engaged`        |
| `nurture`      | Not now. Try again another time.         | `new`            |
| `disqualified` | Not a fit, wrong person, or unreachable. | `disqualified`   |
| `opted_out`    | Asked us to stop.                        | `suppressed`     |

`nurture` returning a prospect to `new` is the point: silence is not rejection, and a prospect who ignored one campaign is a legitimate target for the next.

## How events land

| Event               | Campaign state | Engagement state | Disposition    |
| ------------------- | -------------- | ---------------- | -------------- |
| `enrolled`          | `enrolled`     | —                | —              |
| `message_sent`      | `active`       | —                | —              |
| `message_delivered` | —              | `contacted`      | —              |
| `message_opened`    | —              | —                | —              |
| `message_clicked`   | —              | —                | —              |
| `message_bounced`   | `removed`      | `unreachable`    | `disqualified` |
| `reply_received`    | —              | `responded`      | —              |
| `meeting_booked`    | —              | `responded`      | `interested`   |
| `opted_out`         | `removed`      | —                | `opted_out`    |
| `sequence_finished` | `completed`    | `no_response`    | —              |
| `removed`           | `removed`      | —                | —              |

Two rows deserve attention. `reply_received` sets engagement but no disposition — a reply proves contact, not intent; "no thanks" is a reply too, so a human or a classifier decides between `interested`, `nurture`, and `disqualified`. And `sequence_finished` only forces `no_response` when nothing was heard back; a membership already at `responded` keeps it.

## Prospect status

Status is the prospect's **standing with the organization** — one value, spanning every campaign it has ever been in.

| Status         | Means                                                          |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `new`          | Sourced or nurtured back. Eligible for outbound.               |
| `enrolled`     | Has an open membership in at least one campaign.               |
| `engaged`      | Showed intent. Awaiting promotion; automation paused.          |
| `promoted`     | A `Lead` exists for this person. Outbound must not touch them. |
| `disqualified` | Ruled out — not a fit, wrong person, or unreachable.           |
| `suppressed`   | Must not be contacted. Opt-out or complaint.                   |

```
  ┌─────┐   enrol    ┌──────────┐    interested     ┌─────────┐   promote   ┌──────────┐
  │ new │───────────►│ enrolled │──────────────────►│ engaged │────────────►│ promoted │
  └─────┘◄───────────└──────────┘◄──────────────────└─────────┘◄────────────└──────────┘
     ▲     nurture               re-enrol (nurture)            lead reverted / deleted
     │
     │  manual revive — leaving suppression also requires fresh consent
     │
     ├───────────────────────┐
     │                       │
┌────┴─────────┐      ┌──────┴───────┐
│ disqualified │      │  suppressed  │   ◄── both reachable from any status above
└──────────────┘      └──────────────┘
```

| From           | To             | Trigger                                              |
| -------------- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- |
| `new`          | `enrolled`     | First membership opens.                              |
| `enrolled`     | `new`          | Last membership closed with `nurture`.               |
| `enrolled`     | `engaged`      | A membership closed `interested`.                    |
| `engaged`      | `promoted`     | Promotion creates a `Lead`.                          |
| `engaged`      | `enrolled`     | Re-enrolled instead of promoted.                     |
| `promoted`     | `engaged`      | The lead was reverted or deleted.                    |
| _any_          | `disqualified` | Closed `disqualified`, or judged out of ICP by hand. |
| _any_          | `suppressed`   | Closed `opted_out`, or a spam complaint.             |
| `suppressed`   | `new`          | **Manual only**, and only on recorded fresh consent. |
| `disqualified` | `new`          | **Manual only** — a human decides to revive.         |

Two rules carry weight beyond the table:

1. **Suppression wins.** It is reachable from every status, including `disqualified`, and reversible only by an explicit human action backed by new consent. No campaign event may ever lift it.
2. **Promotion pauses automation.** Entering `promoted` removes the prospect from every open membership. A person being worked by a human is never also being emailed by a sequence.

## Identity: one human, one record

A person sourced twice is one prospect, and a person we already know is not a new prospect at all. Resolution runs on every ingest, in priority order: `apolloPersonId`, then `email`, then `linkedinUrl`, all scoped to the organization.

The outcomes:

- **Matches an existing prospect** → the existing record is enriched. No second prospect, and its campaign history stays whole.
- **Matches an existing `Contact`** → the prospect links to that contact rather than shadowing it. The CRM record stays the source of truth for who this person is; the prospect carries only outbound state.
- **Matches nothing** → a new prospect is created.

A contact we already do business with can legitimately enter a new campaign — that is a new opportunity, not a new person. It produces a new membership against the same linked contact, and on promotion the existing `Contact` and `Company` are reused while a **new `Deal`** is opened. Existing contacts are never duplicated as fresh leads.

## Enrolment eligibility

A prospect may be enrolled only from `new` or `enrolled`. Everything else is blocked, for a different reason each time:

- `engaged` — awaiting a promotion decision; enrolling would talk over the reply.
- `promoted` — a human owns this person now.
- `disqualified` — we decided not to pursue them.
- `suppressed` — we are not allowed to contact them.

Four more gates apply on top of status:

1. **A contactable channel.** At least one channel the campaign can use must not be revoked.
2. **One campaign at a time.** A prospect with an open membership anywhere is blocked from a second. Two cold sequences arriving at once read as spam to the person on the other end, whatever the org chart says.
3. **No open membership in the same campaign.** Enforced in the database by a partial unique index, so a race cannot produce two.
4. **Cooldown.** A membership that closed `nurture` sets `nextEligibleAt` — the date from the reply if one was given, otherwise the default of `DEFAULT_NO_RESPONSE_COOLDOWN_DAYS` (75). Until it passes, enrolment is refused.

Gates 2 and 4 can be overridden by an authorized human passing `force`, which is recorded. **Consent cannot**: `force` never enrols a suppressed prospect, and the MCP tools never expose it at all — an agent gets no override.

## Audit trail

Every event carries `actorId` and `source` (`system`, `user`, `agent`, or `provider`), so the history answers _who_ as well as _what_. A provider webhook records `provider` and no actor; an MCP tool records `agent` with the token's user; the UI records `user`. Concluding a membership writes its own `removed` event, so a disposition set by hand is as traceable as one a campaign produced.

A membership is also bound to the `channel` it runs on, because consent is per channel — an email sequence and a LinkedIn sequence are different conversations with different permissions.

## Channel consent

Consent is tracked **per channel** — `email`, `linkedin`, `phone` — with three states: `unknown`, `granted`, `revoked`.

Outbound is opt-out, not opt-in: `unknown` is contactable. Only `revoked` silences a channel, and it silences only that channel — an email opt-out does not close LinkedIn. A prospect whose every channel is revoked is recorded as `suppressed` outright rather than left to be inferred at send time.

Consent is a permission; `unreachable` is a fact about deliverability. A bounce does not revoke consent, and an opt-out does not mean the address was bad. They are stored separately because they expire differently and answer to different rules.

## Agent surface

Everything here is exposed over the [MCP server](/concepts/mcp-server) under two new scopes, `prospects:read` and `prospects:write`, alongside the existing `leads:*` and `campaigns:*` tools.

| Tool                    | Scope             | Does                                                             |
| ----------------------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `list_prospects`        | `prospects:read`  | Filter by status, campaign, ICP, engagement, or search text.     |
| `get_prospect`          | `prospects:read`  | One prospect with campaign history, consent, and linked contact. |
| `create_prospect`       | `prospects:write` | Add manually; resolves identity before creating.                 |
| `enrol_prospect`        | `prospects:write` | Add to a campaign; refuses when eligibility rules block it.      |
| `record_campaign_event` | `prospects:write` | Append an event and let the derived states follow.               |
| `set_disposition`       | `prospects:write` | Conclude a membership.                                           |
| `promote_prospect`      | `prospects:write` | Promote to a lead and close open memberships.                    |
| `suppress_prospect`     | `prospects:write` | Revoke consent on a channel, or suppress outright.               |

Two rules keep the agent surface honest:

1. **Enum values come from `@zuko/sales`.** Tool schemas build their `z.enum` from the exported constants — a status added here reaches the agent without a second edit, and the two can never drift.
2. **Tools cannot bypass the state machine.** Every write validates through the same `canTransition*` helpers the HTTP API uses, so an agent gets the same refusals a human does — including the manual-only rule on leaving suppression, which an agent cannot satisfy on its own.

## Handoff to the CRM

Promotion creates a `Lead` with status `replied`, the entry point of the existing lead state machine (`replied → interested → not_interested → converted`). The prospect stays in the system at `promoted` and keeps its campaign history — the lead is the working record, the prospect remains the outbound audit trail.

Converting that lead produces a `Contact` (reused when identity resolution already linked one), optionally a `Company`, and a `Deal`. Reverting a converted lead deletes the deal and returns the lead to `replied`; the prospect stays `promoted`. Deleting the lead outright returns the prospect to `engaged`.
